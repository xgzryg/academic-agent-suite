# Methods Directness and Cross-Section Redundancy

Use this rule when drafting, revising, polishing, compressing, or auditing Methods and Supplementary Methods. It controls writing and section placement; it does not validate the statistical method or authorize analytical changes.

## Core decision

Judge propositions in context, not cue words. For each candidate sentence, separate:

- the method fact: who or what was studied, when measurement or follow-up occurred, how a variable or estimand was constructed, what population or model was used, and which rule or threshold governed the analysis;
- the interpretive rhetoric: repeated explanation of why the design was not something else, anticipatory replies to hypothetical reviewers, or reminders telling readers how to interpret a limitation already handled elsewhere.

Delete, tighten, or relocate only the redundant rhetorical layer. If removal would change a reader's understanding of the population, timing, scope, model, variable construction, calculation, or reproducibility, keep the fact or move it to its correct section.

Words such as `because`, `therefore`, `rather than`, `nevertheless`, `however`, and `should be considered` identify candidates only. They are not automatic deletion rules.

## Section ownership

- **Methods and Supplementary Methods:** report actual design, measurement order, follow-up origin, analysis population, variables, covariates, model differences, calculations, thresholds, and screening rules.
- **Results:** report observed patterns and comparisons. Do not place a result judgment such as similarity between cohorts inside a methods paragraph.
- **Discussion:** explain how timing, design, residual uncertainty, or non-causal scope affects interpretation.

Give each fact or limitation one primary home. Repeat it elsewhere only when that section cannot be understood independently without a short reminder. Do not expand the same interpretive caveat in the main Methods, Supplementary Methods, and Discussion merely to pre-empt criticism.

## Drafting and editing limits

1. Prefer direct operational statements over negative or contrastive definitions when they are logically equivalent. If `all` or an explicit formula already defines the complete set, an added “not restricted to” clause is usually redundant.
2. Use `because ... therefore ...`, `rather than ...`, or contrast markers only when the relationship itself changes the study design, analysis scope, or model interpretation. Otherwise state the two relevant facts directly.
3. When one sentence mixes a load-bearing method fact with repeated interpretation, split it: retain the fact in Methods and keep interpretation only in its necessary Discussion location.
4. Remove exact or functional duplicates, including repeated counts or model compositions, after confirming that one complete and correctly placed statement remains.
5. Preserve all load-bearing content: measurement and follow-up timing; inclusion, exclusion, and screening rules; cohort source and overlap; covariates and coding; real differences among models; estimands, simulations, imputation, pooling, or standardization; multiplicity families; figure-display filters; and reasons that determine analytic counts or event ordering.
6. Do not turn a style edit into a scientific correction. A clearer sentence does not resolve a statistical, causal, temporal, or model-validity problem; report any such issue separately.
7. Respect task authority. A read-only audit reports candidates and deliberate `KEEP` decisions without editing files. An authorized local edit changes only the stated scope and records any dependency that remains outside it.

## Application by task

- **New Methods drafting:** prevent defensive repetition at first draft while keeping every known reproducibility fact.
- **Polishing or compression:** read the necessary Methods, Supplementary Methods, Results, and Discussion context before deleting a repeated limitation; never remove all surviving locations of a load-bearing fact.
- **Style audit:** distinguish rhetorical defensiveness, ordinary redundancy, and required method information. Do not manufacture findings from transition words.
- **Final check:** confirm that each edited passage retains scientific meaning and that factual method statements remain in Methods while interpretive consequences remain available in Discussion.

Project-specific entities, sample counts, variable names, and analysis choices belong in the current manuscript or project lock, not in this reusable rule.
