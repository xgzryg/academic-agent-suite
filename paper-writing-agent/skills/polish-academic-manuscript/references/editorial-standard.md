# Editorial Standard

Use this standard for language polishing. Preserve the author's scientific content and recognizable voice; improve only what materially benefits correctness, precision, coherence, or journal readiness.

## Core principles

### Clarity

- Prefer unambiguous, direct constructions.
- Make pronoun and modifier referents explicit when the source supports the clarification.
- Avoid ornamental vocabulary and unnecessary synonym substitution.
- Retain technical terms that are standard in the relevant discipline.

### Logical flow and coherence

- Preserve the original argument and section structure.
- Make sentence-to-sentence relationships clear with restrained transitions.
- Maintain given-to-new information flow and consistent topic focus within paragraphs.
- Remove logical connectors that overstate causality, contrast, or inference.

### Precision

- Preserve the distinction among observation, association, prediction, mechanism, and causation.
- Retain calibrated uncertainty: for example, do not turn `may suggest` into `demonstrates`.
- Use exact referents and consistent technical terminology.
- Preserve mathematical, statistical, and scientific notation.

### Concision

- Remove redundancy, empty lead-ins, inflated phrasing, and avoidable nominalizations.
- Prefer shorter wording only when meaning, emphasis, and logical scope remain intact.
- Never shorten by deleting qualifications, methodological details, limitations, data, or citations.

### Formal academic style

- Replace colloquial, promotional, emotional, or vague wording with neutral scholarly prose.
- Avoid unsupported superlatives and novelty claims.
- Use first person, passive voice, and active voice according to disciplinary and journal norms; do not apply a blanket ban.
- Avoid contractions unless the target venue permits them.

### Grammar and mechanics

- Correct agreement, tense, articles, prepositions, countability, comparison, parallelism, punctuation, and sentence boundaries.
- Place modifiers next to what they modify.
- Maintain consistent serial-comma, quotation-mark, capitalization, hyphenation, and number styles.
- Use Unicode symbols or spelled-out forms consistently with the source and target format.

### Consistency

- Define each nonstandard abbreviation at first appropriate use in the main text and use it consistently thereafter; respect separate abstract conventions.
- Standardize names for variables, groups, interventions, datasets, instruments, species, genes/proteins, and software only when the intended identity is certain.
- Preserve SI units and discipline-specific nomenclature.
- Keep citation and cross-reference syntax internally consistent without inventing content.

## Section-specific guidance

### Title

- Make the title accurate, concise, searchable, and grammatically complete for its chosen form.
- Preserve study design, population, intervention/exposure, and scope when they are central.
- Do not add claims of novelty, mechanism, causation, or generality.

### Abstract

- Preserve the abstract's required structure and word-limit awareness.
- Make the objective, methods, principal results, and conclusion easy to identify.
- Keep key numerical findings and uncertainty measures unchanged.
- Ensure the conclusion does not exceed the evidence reported in the abstract or body.

### Keywords

- Preserve the number and ordering unless the user or journal requirements say otherwise.
- Standardize capitalization and separators.
- Do not invent indexing terms without authorization.

### Introduction

- Clarify the progression from background to knowledge gap, rationale, and study objective.
- Avoid rewriting the literature narrative into claims stronger than the cited evidence.
- Keep contribution statements specific and proportionate.

### Methods

- Prioritize reproducibility and chronological or procedural clarity.
- Preserve every parameter, material, version, threshold, inclusion criterion, and ethical statement.
- Use tense consistently with completed procedures and established methods.
- Do not repair an apparently incomplete method by inventing details.

### Results

- Report findings neutrally and in alignment with tables and figures.
- Preserve all values, signs, decimal places, uncertainty intervals, P values, effect measures, and group labels.
- Separate observed results from interpretation when the source structure already does so.

### Discussion

- Clarify the sequence of principal findings, interpretation, comparison with prior work, implications, strengths, limitations, and future directions.
- Preserve qualifiers and alternative explanations.
- Remove repetition of results only when no substantive nuance is lost.

### Conclusion

- Keep the conclusion proportional to the study design and results.
- Avoid new evidence, new citations, or expanded generalizations.
- Retain limitations or conditions essential to interpreting the conclusion.

### Captions and tables

- Make captions self-contained only to the degree supported by the source.
- Preserve identifiers, panel labels, footnote symbols, units, values, and cell alignment.
- Polish prose labels consistently without changing data or table structure.

### Declarations and references

- Preserve legal, ethical, funding, authorship, conflict-of-interest, data-availability, and consent statements unless the user explicitly authorizes substantive changes.
- Do not rewrite reference titles or infer metadata. Limit changes to safe formatting normalization requested by the user or target style.

## Manuscript-wide QA questions

Before delivery, answer these privately from the final file:

1. Does the title accurately match the study presented?
2. Do the abstract and conclusion agree with the body?
3. Is the research objective stated consistently?
4. Are technical terms and abbreviations consistent and defined appropriately?
5. Are causal and certainty claims proportionate to the design and evidence?
6. Are all numerical and citation-bearing elements preserved?
7. Has any substantive source content been omitted, duplicated, or moved?
8. Is the English variant and editorial style consistent?
9. Is every document object or Markdown block intact?
10. Does the output contain only manuscript content?
