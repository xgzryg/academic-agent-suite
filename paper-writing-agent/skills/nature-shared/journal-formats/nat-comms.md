# Nature Communications: article requirements and stage-aware checks

Verified source snapshot: 2026-09-11. The current [Article page](https://www.nature.com/ncomms/submit/article)
supports these Article limits: counted main text ideally 5,000 words, excluding
Abstract, Methods, References and figure legends; title at most 15 words;
unreferenced abstract at most 200 words; Methods typically below 3,000 words;
legends at most 350 words each; references normally no more than 70; up to 10
figures/tables according to length, with at most four below 2,000 main-text words.
Use Introduction, Results, Discussion when appropriate, and Methods when
appropriate. Results/Methods can have topical subheadings; Discussion is succinct
and without them. Recheck the live page before a later submission or when an
editor supplies different instructions.

The [content-type page](https://www.nature.com/ncomms/submit/content-types)
uses Articles for original research of varying length and separately describes
Registered Reports, invited Reviews/Perspectives/Comments and Matters Arising.
Do not apply the old local table of Brief Communication or Correspondence limits.

## Drafting and polishing

Build a word budget around the requested material; Methods is not inside the
5,000-word main-text count. Preserve reproducibility and conclusion-changing
evidence. The abstract should give a brief accessible topic introduction,
main findings and implications; do not enforce a finding-first opening or
force a number that does not materially support the main claim.

For a first-submission draft, missing optional preferences need not block writing.
Use placeholders for missing author facts. A concise cover letter should explain
the actual result, contribution and audience fit; three functions do not imply
exactly three sentences. Declarations remain truthful and author-specific.

## Additional live checks when applicable

Use the [author hub](https://www.nature.com/ncomms/submit) to find current
submission, formatting, data/code, reporting, image and policy pages. Check only
items relevant to the requested stage:

- submission file types, anonymization and cover-page information;
- display readability, panel labels, size, resolution and permitted file formats;
- citation ordering/style and reference metadata;
- Supplementary Information organization and source-data access;
- Data/Code availability, reviewer access, repository identifiers and release timing;
- author contributions, competing interests, funding, ethics and applicable reporting forms;
- cover-letter disclosures, related manuscripts and transfers;
- current open-access fee, licence and peer-review publication/opt-out rules if requested.

Initial-review access, acceptance and public-release obligations can differ.
Do not declare a valid embargoed/restricted-access record invalid merely because
it is not yet public; establish the applicable stage and policy. A script or
repository DOI is not mandatory solely because an old local example used one.
If current pages are unavailable, report the affected requirement as unverified
and continue work that does not depend on it. Do not invent a current fee,
template requirement or production blocker.
